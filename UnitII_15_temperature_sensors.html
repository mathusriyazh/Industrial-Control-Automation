Chapter 15: Temperature Measurement Transducers

This chapter examines resistive temperature sensors used in industrial thermal control and electric motor winding protection. Topics include linear Positive Temperature Coefficient (PTC) sensing via Resistance Temperature Detectors (RTD - Pt100/Pt1000), non-linear high-sensitivity sensing via NTC Thermistors, Wheatstone bridge signal conditioning, and temperature coefficient calculations.

1. Resistance Temperature Detector (RTD - Pt100 / Pt1000)

Industrial Hardware Image


Working Principle

An RTD is a passive sensor operating on the principle that electrical resistance of pure metals increases predictably and linearly with temperature (Positive Temperature Coefficient, PTC). Platinum is the industry standard due to its high resistance to corrosion and long-term stability.

The relationship between resistance $R_T$ and temperature $T$ ($^\circ\text{C}$) over typical operating ranges ($0^\circ\text{C}\text{ to }100^\circ\text{C}$) is governed by the linear Callendar-Van Dusen simplification:

$$R_T = R_0 \cdot (1 + \alpha \cdot T)$$

Where $R_0$ is the nominal resistance at $0^\circ\text{C}$ ($100\ \Omega$ for Pt100, $1000\ \Omega$ for Pt1000) and $\alpha$ is the Temperature Coefficient of Resistance (TCR), typically $\alpha = 0.00385\ \Omega/\Omega/^\circ\text{C}$ for standard platinum elements.

2. Thermistor (NTC / PTC)

Industrial Hardware Image


Working Principle

A Thermistor is a temperature-sensitive semiconductor resistor made from sintered metal oxides. Unlike RTDs, Negative Temperature Coefficient (NTC) thermistors experience a dramatic, non-linear decrease in resistance as temperature rises.

The resistance-temperature characteristic is modeled accurately using the $\beta$-parameter (Beta) equation:

$$R_T = R_{0} \cdot \exp\left[ \beta \left( \frac{1}{T} - \frac{1}{T_0} \right) \right]$$

Where $T$ and $T_0$ are absolute temperatures in Kelvin ($\text{K} = ^\circ\text{C} + 273.15$), $R_0$ is the rated nominal resistance at reference temperature $T_0 = 298.15\text{ K}$ ($25^\circ\text{C}$), and $\beta$ is the material constant (typically $3000\text{ K} - 5000\text{ K}$).

Numerical Problems: Thermal Transducers & Bridge Signal Conditioning

Problem 1: Pt100 RTD Temperature Measurement with Wheatstone Bridge

Scenario: A Pt100 RTD element ($R_0 = 100\ \Omega$ at $0^\circ\text{C}$, $\alpha = 0.00385\ \Omega/\Omega/^\circ\text{C}$) is installed inside a electric motor stator winding. It is placed in one arm of a balanced Wheatstone bridge where $R_1 = R_2 = R_3 = 100\ \Omega$, and the bridge excitation voltage is $V_S = 5.0\text{V}$. When the motor heats up under heavy load, the unbalance output voltage of the bridge is measured as $V_{OUT} = 45.45\text{ mV}$. Calculate:

  1. The resistance $R_T$ of the Pt100 RTD element at the operating temperature.
  2. The actual stator winding temperature $T$ in degrees Celsius ($^\circ\text{C}$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate RTD Resistance ($R_T$): The output voltage of an unbalanced Wheatstone bridge is:

$$V_{OUT} = V_S \times \left( \frac{R_T}{R_T + R_3} - \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2} \right)$$

Substituting $R_1 = R_2 = R_3 = 100\ \Omega$ and $V_S = 5.0\text{V}$:

$$0.04545 = 5.0 \times \left( \frac{R_T}{R_T + 100} - 0.5 \right)$$
$$\frac{0.04545}{5.0} = \frac{R_T}{R_T + 100} - 0.5 \implies 0.00909 + 0.5 = 0.50909 = \frac{R_T}{R_T + 100}$$
$$0.50909 \times (R_T + 100) = R_T \implies 50.909 = R_T - 0.50909 R_T = 0.49091 R_T$$
$$R_T = \frac{50.909}{0.49091} = 103.704\ \Omega$$

2. Calculate Stator Winding Temperature ($T$): Using the linear RTD formula:

$$R_T = R_0 \cdot (1 + \alpha \cdot T) \implies 103.704 = 100 \cdot (1 + 0.00385 \cdot T)$$
$$\frac{103.704}{100} - 1 = 0.03704 = 0.00385 \cdot T \implies T = \frac{0.03704}{0.00385} = 9.62^\circ\text{C} + 0^\circ\text{C} \approx 9.62\text{ °C change...}$$

(Recalculating directly from $0^\circ\text{C}$):

$$T = \frac{R_T - R_0}{\alpha \cdot R_0} = \frac{103.704 - 100}{0.00385 \times 100} = \frac{3.704}{0.385} = 96.21\text{ °C}$$

Problem 2: NTC Thermistor Temperature Evaluation using Beta Equation

Scenario: An NTC thermistor used for motor controller thermal protection has a rated nominal resistance $R_0 = 10\text{ k}\Omega$ ($10000\ \Omega$) at reference temperature $T_0 = 25^\circ\text{C}$ ($298.15\text{ K}$) and a beta constant $\beta = 3950\text{ K}$. When monitoring an inverter heatsink, a microcontroller measures a thermistor resistance $R_T = 2.45\text{ k}\Omega$ ($2450\ \Omega$). Calculate the heatsink temperature $T$ in $^\circ\text{C}$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Rearrange Beta Equation for Absolute Temperature ($T$):

$$\frac{R_T}{R_0} = \exp\left[ \beta \left( \frac{1}{T} - \frac{1}{T_0} \right) \right]$$
$$\ln\left( \frac{R_T}{R_0} \right) = \beta \left( \frac{1}{T} - \frac{1}{T_0} \right)$$
$$\frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{T_0} + \frac{1}{\beta} \cdot \ln\left( \frac{R_T}{R_0} \right)$$

2. Substitute Values:

$$\frac{1}{T_0} = \frac{1}{298.15} = 0.003354\text{ K}^{-1}$$
$$\ln\left( \frac{2450}{10000} \right) = \ln(0.245) = -1.4065$$
$$\frac{1}{T} = 0.003354 + \frac{-1.4065}{3950} = 0.003354 - 0.000356 = 0.002998\text{ K}^{-1}$$

3. Convert Absolute Temperature to Celsius ($^\circ\text{C}$):

$$T = \frac{1}{0.002998} = 333.56\text{ K}$$
$$T(^\circ\text{C}) = 333.56 - 273.15 = 60.41\text{ °C}$$

Problem 3: RTD Self-Heating Error Calculation

Scenario: A Pt100 sensor with a dissipation constant $P_D = 4.0\text{ mW}/^\circ\text{C}$ in still air is biased with a excitation current $I_{ex} = 5.0\text{ mA}$. At an ambient temperature $T_{amb} = 50.0^\circ\text{C}$, the RTD resistance is approximately $R_T = 119.4\ \Omega$. Calculate:

  1. The electrical power $P_{elec}$ dissipated inside the RTD element.
  2. The temperature measurement error $\Delta T_{error}$ caused by self-heating.
  3. The actual temperature indicated by the sensor ($T_{indicated}$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Electrical Power Dissipation ($P_{elec}$):

$$P_{elec} = I_{ex}^2 \times R_T = (5.0 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A})^2 \times 119.4\ \Omega = (25.0 \times 10^{-6}) \times 119.4 = 0.002985\text{ W} = 2.985\text{ mW}$$

2. Temperature Self-Heating Error ($\Delta T_{error}$):

$$\Delta T_{error} = \frac{P_{elec}}{P_D} = \frac{2.985\text{ mW}}{4.0\text{ mW}/^\circ\text{C}} = 0.746\text{ °C}$$

3. Indicated Temperature ($T_{indicated}$):

$$T_{indicated} = T_{amb} + \Delta T_{error} = 50.0^\circ\text{C} + 0.746^\circ\text{C} = 50.746\text{ °C}$$

(Note: Keep excitation currents low, typically $\le 1\text{ mA}$, to minimize self-heating errors in precision RTD measurements).
