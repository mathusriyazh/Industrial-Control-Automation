Chapter 17: Liquid Level Sensing – Float Switch Transducers

This chapter covers fluid level monitoring techniques in tanks and industrial vessels. Topics include buoyant force dynamics based on Archimedes' Principle, magnetic reed switch actuation, continuous vs. discrete level control, pump hysteresis limits, and sizing calculations.

1. Float Switch Transducer (Magnetic Reed & Cable Tilt Types)

Industrial Hardware Image


Working Principle

A float switch operates on buoyancy, where an object less dense than the liquid rests on its surface. As liquid level changes, the floating body shifts vertically along a guide stem or tilts at a fixed cable anchor point.

The buoyancy force $F_b$ sustaining the float is defined by Archimedes' Principle:

$$F_b = \rho_{liquid} \cdot V_{submerged} \cdot g$$

Where $\rho_{liquid}$ is fluid density, $V_{submerged}$ is the displaced fluid volume, and $g$ is gravitational acceleration ($9.81\text{ m/s}^2$). For vertical magnetic float switches, an internal permanent magnet inside the floating element actuates a hermetically sealed reed switch inside the central shaft when passing the set threshold level, changing the electrical contact state (NO/NC).

2. Level Control Systems & Hysteresis

Dual-Point Pump Control (Latch Circuit)

Industrial sumps and storage reservoirs use dual float switch positions (or a single cable-tilt float with configurable mechanical differential) to establish a hysteresis band ($\Delta h$).

This prevents rapid cycling (chattering) of pump motors. The high limit setpoint ($h_{HIGH}$) triggers the discharge pump ON, while the low limit setpoint ($h_{LOW}$) turns the pump OFF:

$$\Delta h = h_{HIGH} - h_{LOW}$$
$$V_{buffer} = A_{tank} \times \Delta h = A_{tank} \times (h_{HIGH} - h_{LOW})$$

Where $A_{tank}$ is the cross-sectional area of the tank and $V_{buffer}$ is the controllable fluid buffer volume.

Numerical Problems: Buoyancy, Buffer Volumes & Level Sizing

Problem 1: Float Buoyancy and Displaced Volume

Scenario: A cylindrical stainless steel float for a level switch has a total mass $m = 180\text{ grams}$ ($0.180\text{ kg}$) and a total outer volume $V_{total} = 400\text{ cm}^3$ ($4.0 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^3$). It operates in a oil storage tank containing hydraulic oil with a specific density $\rho_{oil} = 850\text{ kg/m}^3$. Calculate:

  1. The total gravitational force (weight) $W$ acting downwards on the float.
  2. The submerged volume $V_{submerged}$ required for the float to achieve neutral equilibrium (float on the surface).
  3. The percentage of total float volume submerged ($\% V_{submerged}$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate Downward Gravitational Force ($W$):

$$W = m \cdot g = 0.180\text{ kg} \times 9.81\text{ m/s}^2 = 1.7658\text{ N}$$

2. Calculate Required Displaced Volume ($V_{submerged}$): At floating equilibrium, $F_b = W$:

$$F_b = \rho_{oil} \cdot V_{submerged} \cdot g = W$$
$$V_{submerged} = \frac{W}{\rho_{oil} \cdot g} = \frac{1.7658\text{ N}}{850\text{ kg/m}^3 \times 9.81\text{ m/s}^2} = \frac{1.7658}{8338.5} = 2.1176 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^3 = 211.76\text{ cm}^3$$

3. Calculate Percentage Submerged:

$$\% V_{submerged} = \left( \frac{V_{submerged}}{V_{total}} \right) \times 100 = \left( \frac{211.76\text{ cm}^3}{400\text{ cm}^3} \right) \times 100 = 52.94\%$$

Problem 2: Dual Float Switch Hysteresis and Pump Cycle Frequency

Scenario: An industrial wastewater sump has a cylindrical cross-section with an inner diameter $D = 1.6\text{ meters}$. Two float switches set the level limits: Low Limit $h_{LOW} = 0.5\text{ m}$ and High Limit $h_{HIGH} = 2.0\text{ m}$. Water flows continuously into the sump at an inflow rate $Q_{in} = 120\text{ L/min}$ ($0.120\text{ m}^3/\text{min}$). When the high float triggers, a discharge pump empties the tank at a rated flow rate $Q_{pump} = 420\text{ L/min}$ ($0.420\text{ m}^3/\text{min}$). Calculate:

  1. The active liquid buffer volume $V_{buffer}$ between float setpoints.
  2. The time required to fill the tank from $h_{LOW}$ to $h_{HIGH}$ with the pump OFF ($t_{fill}$).
  3. The time required to drain the tank from $h_{HIGH}$ to $h_{LOW}$ with the pump ON ($t_{drain}$).
  4. The total pump cycle period ($T_{total}$) and duty cycle percentage.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate Active Buffer Volume ($V_{buffer}$):

$$A_{tank} = \frac{\pi \cdot D^2}{4} = \frac{\pi \cdot (1.6)^2}{4} = 2.0106\text{ m}^2$$
$$\Delta h = h_{HIGH} - h_{LOW} = 2.0\text{ m} - 0.5\text{ m} = 1.5\text{ m}$$
$$V_{buffer} = A_{tank} \times \Delta h = 2.0106\text{ m}^2 \times 1.5\text{ m} = 3.0159\text{ m}^3 = 3015.9\text{ Liters}$$

2. Calculate Fill Time ($t_{fill}$): (Pump is OFF, only inflow active)

$$t_{fill} = \frac{V_{buffer}}{Q_{in}} = \frac{3.0159\text{ m}^3}{0.120\text{ m}^3/\text{min}} = 25.13\text{ minutes}$$

3. Calculate Drain Time ($t_{drain}$): (Pump is ON, net outflow is $Q_{pump} - Q_{in}$)

$$Q_{net\_out} = Q_{pump} - Q_{in} = 0.420 - 0.120 = 0.300\text{ m}^3/\text{min}$$
$$t_{drain} = \frac{V_{buffer}}{Q_{net\_out}} = \frac{3.0159\text{ m}^3}{0.300\text{ m}^3/\text{min}} = 10.05\text{ minutes}$$

4. Calculate Total Period ($T_{total}$) and Duty Cycle:

$$T_{total} = t_{fill} + t_{drain} = 25.13\text{ min} + 10.05\text{ min} = 35.18\text{ minutes}$$
$$\text{Duty Cycle} = \left( \frac{t_{drain}}{T_{total}} \right) \times 100 = \left( \frac{10.05}{35.18} \right) \times 100 \approx 28.57\%$$

Problem 3: Hydrostatic Pressure Equivalent Verification

Scenario: A backup hydrostatic pressure sensor is installed at the bottom of a water tank ($1000\text{ kg/m}^3$) to cross-verify float switch trigger points. If the High-Level float switch is positioned at a height $h = 3.2\text{ meters}$ above the sensor tap, calculate the expected hydrostatic gauge pressure $P_{gauge}$ in kilopascals ($\text{kPa}$) and bar when water reaches the switch.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate Hydrostatic Pressure ($P_{gauge}$):

$$P_{gauge} = \rho \cdot g \cdot h = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3 \times 9.81\text{ m/s}^2 \times 3.2\text{ m}$$
$$P_{gauge} = 31392\text{ Pa} = 31.392\text{ kPa}$$

2. Convert to Bar ($1\text{ bar} = 100\text{ kPa}$):

$$P_{gauge} = \frac{31.392\text{ kPa}}{100\text{ kPa/bar}} = 0.3139\text{ bar}$$
