1. Closed-Loop Servo Motor Actuators

Industrial Hardware Image


Working Principle

A servo motor actuator is a closed-loop electromechanical system comprising an electric motor, a high-resolution feedback transducer (rotary encoder or resolver), drive electronics, and a controller. Unlike open-loop stepper motors, servos continuously compare actual rotor position $\theta(t)$ against a commanded reference $\theta_{ref}(t)$.

Electromagnetic torque $T_m$ generated by the motor rotor is proportional to phase current $I_q$ and permanent magnet field flux $\Phi$:

$$T_m = K_t \cdot I_q$$

Where $K_t$ is the motor torque constant ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m/A}$). The dynamic rotational equation governing rotor speed $\omega$ and position $\theta$ under load torque $T_L$ and viscous friction $B$ is:

$$J \cdot \frac{d\omega}{dt} + B \cdot \omega = T_m - T_L = K_t \cdot I_q - T_L$$

Position error $e(t) = \theta_{ref}(t) - \theta(t)$ is processed by a proportional-integral-derivative (PID) servo drive loop to modulate pulse-width modulation (PWM) power delivered to the stator windings.

2. Gearhead Dynamics & Mechanical Power Transfer

Gear Reduction Ratio & Inertia Reflection

High-speed, low-torque servo motors are frequently paired with precision planetary gearheads of gear ratio $N:1$ ($N = \frac{\omega_{in}}{\omega_{out}} > 1$) to increase output torque and match load inertia.

The output torque $T_{out}$ delivered to the load increases proportionally to gear ratio $N$ and mechanical efficiency $\eta$:

$$T_{out} = N \cdot \eta \cdot T_m$$

The load inertia $J_L$ reflected back to the motor shaft is reduced quadratically by the square of the gear ratio $N^2$:

$$J_{reflected} = \frac{J_L}{N^2}$$

Total inertia $J_{total}$ seen by the motor rotor is given by $J_{total} = J_m + \frac{J_L}{N^2}$. Optimal servo acceleration occurs when motor inertia matches reflected load inertia ($J_m \approx J_{reflected}$).

Numerical Problems: Servo Torque, Acceleration & Encoder Resolution

Problem 1: Servo Motor Angular Acceleration & Torque Balance

Scenario: A direct-drive brushless AC servo motor has a torque constant $K_t = 0.85\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/A}$ and rotor inertia $J_m = 0.0012\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$. It is coupled to a rotational load with inertia $J_L = 0.0036\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$. Friction load is $T_L = 0.50\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.

Calculate:

  1. The total system inertia $J_{total}$ connected to the motor.
  2. The electromagnetic torque $T_m$ generated when the drive supplies a peak phase current $I_q = 12.0\text{ A}$.
  3. The net angular acceleration $\alpha$ in radians per second squared ($\text{rad/s}^2$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate Total System Inertia ($J_{total}$):

$$J_{total} = J_m + J_L = 0.0012\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2 + 0.0036\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2 = 0.0048\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$$

2. Calculate Electromagnetic Torque ($T_m$):

$$T_m = K_t \cdot I_q = 0.85\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/A} \times 12.0\text{ A} = 10.20\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$

3. Calculate Angular Acceleration ($\alpha$): Applying Newton's Second Law for rotation ($T_{net} = J_{total} \cdot \alpha$):

$$T_{net} = T_m - T_L = 10.20\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} - 0.50\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = 9.70\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
$$\alpha = \frac{T_{net}}{J_{total}} = \frac{9.70\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}{0.0048\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2} = 2020.83\text{ rad/s}^2$$

Problem 2: Gearhead Torque Amplification & Reflected Inertia

Scenario: A servo motor producing a continuous rated torque $T_m = 2.4\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ at $3000\text{ RPM}$ is mated to a planetary gearhead with a speed reduction ratio $N = 10:1$ and mechanical efficiency $\eta = 92\%$ ($0.92$). The load inertia attached to the gearbox output shaft is $J_L = 0.25\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$.

Calculate:

  1. The continuous torque $T_{out}$ available at the gearbox output shaft.
  2. The rotational speed $\omega_{out}$ of the gearbox output shaft in $\text{RPM}$.
  3. The load inertia $J_{reflected}$ reflected back to the motor shaft.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate Gearbox Output Torque ($T_{out}$):

$$T_{out} = N \cdot \eta \cdot T_m = 10 \times 0.92 \times 2.4\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = 22.08\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$

2. Calculate Output Speed ($\omega_{out}$):

$$\omega_{out} = \frac{\omega_{in}}{N} = \frac{3000\text{ RPM}}{10} = 300\text{ RPM}$$

3. Calculate Reflected Load Inertia ($J_{reflected}$):

$$J_{reflected} = \frac{J_L}{N^2} = \frac{0.25\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2}{10^2} = \frac{0.25}{100} = 0.0025\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$$

Problem 3: Incremental Quadrature Encoder Resolution

Scenario: A precision servo motor uses a 2048 PPR (Pulses Per Revolution) quadrature optical encoder on its shaft. The motor drives a ball screw linear actuator with a pitch $L = 5\text{ mm/rev}$ ($0.005\text{ m/rev}$).

Determine:

  1. The total count resolution per revolution using $4\times$ quadrature decoding ($CPR$).
  2. The angular position resolution $\Delta \theta$ in degrees per encoder count.
  3. The theoretical linear positioning resolution $\Delta x$ of the ball screw stage in micrometers ($\mu\text{m}$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate Quadrature Counts Per Revolution ($CPR$):

$$CPR = \text{PPR} \times 4 = 2048 \times 4 = 8192\text{ counts/rev}$$

2. Calculate Angular Position Resolution ($\Delta \theta$):

$$\Delta \theta = \frac{360^\circ}{CPR} = \frac{360^\circ}{8192} = 0.04395^\circ\text{ / count}$$

3. Calculate Linear Position Resolution ($\Delta x$):

$$\Delta x = \frac{L}{CPR} = \frac{5\text{ mm}}{8192\text{ counts}} = 0.00061035\text{ mm/count} = 0.6104\ \mu\text{m/count}$$
