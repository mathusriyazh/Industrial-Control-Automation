1. Need for Industrial Automation

Industrial production was traditionally performed mainly by human operators. As production requirements increased, industries started using machines, controllers, sensors, actuators and communication systems to automate repetitive and complex operations.

Central idea: Automation is required when an industry needs to produce more products, maintain consistent quality, improve safety, reduce production time and continuously monitor the process.

2. Automation in Modern Manufacturing

Modern factories use robots, conveyors, sensors, PLCs, industrial networks, machine vision systems and SCADA/HMI systems to coordinate production.

Real industrial automation example.
Observe: A modern automated production system does not depend only on a single machine. Several machines, sensors, controllers and communication systems work together.

3. Why Do We Need Automation?

### 3.1 Increased Productivity Automation allows machines to perform repetitive operations at a high speed and with consistent cycle time.
### 3.2 Improved Quality Automated machines can maintain consistent position, speed, temperature, pressure, force and timing.
### 3.3 Improved Safety Automation can keep operators away from hazardous temperature, high voltage, moving machinery and chemicals.
### 3.4 Reduced Human Error A programmed controller executes the same sequence repeatedly without fatigue.
### 3.5 Reduced Production Time Machines can operate with precise and repeatable cycle times.
### 3.6 Real-Time Monitoring Sensors continuously provide information about the condition of the machine or process.
### 3.7 Better Resource Utilization Automation can optimize energy, material, machine time and manpower.
### 3.8 Continuous Operation Automated systems can operate for long periods with limited human intervention, subject to maintenance and safety requirements.

7. Productivity and Automation

Productivity describes how effectively resources such as time, machines, materials and manpower are converted into useful products.

Basic productivity expression:

Productivity = Output / Input

Example

Suppose a manual production line produces 100 units in 8 hours.

Productivity = 100 / 8

= 12.5 units/hour

After automation, suppose the line produces 240 units in the same 8 hours.

Productivity = 240 / 8

= 30 units/hour

The production rate has increased from 12.5 units/hour to 30 units/hour.

8. Automation and Quality

One major reason for automation is to obtain consistent product quality.

Position

Servo systems can position a machine accurately.

Temperature

Sensors and controllers can maintain a specified temperature.

Speed

VFDs and motor controllers maintain required motor speed.

Important: Automation does not automatically guarantee perfect quality. Poor sensor calibration, incorrect programming, mechanical wear and communication failures can also produce defective products.

11. Does Automation Always Reduce Cost?

Automation generally requires significant initial investment. The system may require PLCs, sensors, actuators, drives, industrial networks, robots, software and engineering.

However, automation can reduce recurring costs through:

  • Higher production rate
  • Reduced scrap
  • Reduced downtime
  • Improved machine utilization
  • Reduced repetitive manual work
  • Predictable production cycles
  • Improved process monitoring
Engineering decision: Automation should not be selected simply because it is "automatic". The engineer should compare the required investment with productivity, quality, safety, flexibility and long-term operating requirements.

13. Limitations and Challenges

High initial investment
Automation equipment, software, sensors and engineering can require significant capital expenditure.
Maintenance requirement
Sensors, drives, controllers and communication networks require periodic maintenance.
Skilled personnel
Engineers and technicians need knowledge of PLC programming, instrumentation, drives, networking and troubleshooting.
Cybersecurity
Connected industrial systems require appropriate cybersecurity measures.
System failure
A failure in a critical controller, sensor, network or actuator can interrupt production.

15. Chapter Summary

  • Automation increases production capability.
  • Automation improves repeatability and consistency.
  • Automation can improve industrial safety.
  • Sensors provide real-time process information.
  • Controllers execute the required control logic.
  • Actuators perform the physical operation.
  • HMI and SCADA allow operators to monitor and interact with industrial processes.
  • Automation can reduce production time and operating cost, but requires initial investment and skilled maintenance.
  • Industrial automation should be designed according to process requirements rather than simply maximizing automation.
