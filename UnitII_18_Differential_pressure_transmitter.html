Chapter 18: Differential Pressure (DP) Transmitters

This chapter examines industrial Differential Pressure (DP) transmitters used for measuring pressure drop across flow primary elements, closed-tank liquid levels, and filter degradation. Topics include high/low pressure ports ($P_H, P_L$), internal sensing cell mechanics, square-root extraction for flow rate, 4–20 mA current loop conversion, and wet/dry leg installations.

1. Differential Pressure (DP) Transmitter Fundamentals

Industrial Hardware Image


Working Principle

A Differential Pressure (DP) Transmitter measures the difference between two process pressures applied to its High-Pressure ($P_H$) and Low-Pressure ($P_L$) ports. The net differential pressure is defined as:

$$\Delta P = P_H - P_L$$

Process fluid exerts force on dual flexible isolating diaphragms. A fill fluid (typically silicone oil or fluorolube) transfers this differential force to a central sensing diaphragm (piezoresistive or capacitive cell). The resulting deflection alters capacitance or resistance proportional to $\Delta P$. Electronics then condition this into a standardized 4–20 mA DC industrial current loop signal.

When used with primary flow elements like an Orifice Plate, fluid flow velocity ($Q$) relates to differential pressure according to Bernoulli's Principle:

$$Q = K \cdot \sqrt{\Delta P} = K \cdot \sqrt{P_H - P_L}$$

2. Signal Scaling & Closed-Tank Level Measurement

Square-Root Extraction & 4–20 mA Output

For differential pressure flow measurement, the raw signal from the DP cell is non-linear ($\Delta P \propto Q^2$). Modern smart transmitters apply internal Square-Root Extraction to linearize the 4–20 mA current output:

$$I_{out} = 4\text{ mA} + 16\text{ mA} \times \sqrt{\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta P_{max}}}$$

In pressurized tank level applications, a **Wet Leg** or **Dry Leg** compensates for upper vapor pressure ($P_{gas}$):

$$\Delta P = P_H - P_L = (\rho_{liquid} \cdot g \cdot h + P_{gas}) - P_{gas} = \rho_{liquid} \cdot g \cdot h$$

Numerical Problems: Differential Pressure & Current Loop Scaling

Problem 1: DP Flowmeter Current Loop Output Calculation

Scenario: A Differential Pressure transmitter is calibrated across an Orifice Plate to measure water flow from $0$ to $500\text{ m}^3/\text{h}$, corresponding to a full-scale differential pressure range of $0$ to $100\text{ kPa}$. The transmitter utilizes built-in Square-Root Extraction and outputs a standard $4\text{--}20\text{ mA}$ signal.

Calculate the transmitter output current $I_{out}$ when the measured differential pressure is $\Delta P = 36\text{ kPa}$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate Normalized DP Ratio ($\Delta P / \Delta P_{max}$):

$$\text{Ratio} = \frac{36\text{ kPa}}{100\text{ kPa}} = 0.36$$

2. Apply Square-Root Linearization for Flow Rate:

$$\text{Flow Ratio} = \sqrt{\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta P_{max}}} = \sqrt{0.36} = 0.60 \quad (60\%\text{ of max flow})$$

3. Calculate 4–20 mA Output Current ($I_{out}$):

$$I_{out} = I_{min} + (I_{max} - I_{min}) \times \text{Flow Ratio}$$
$$I_{out} = 4.0\text{ mA} + (16.0\text{ mA}) \times 0.60 = 4.0 + 9.6 = 13.6\text{ mA}$$

Problem 2: Pressurized Boiler Level with Wet-Leg Compensation

Scenario: A pressurized steam boiler vessel operates with a wet leg connected to the low-pressure port ($P_L$) filled with condensate water ($\rho_{ref} = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3$) up to a fixed height $H = 4.0\text{ meters}$. The High-Pressure port ($P_H$) connects to the bottom of the boiler, where process fluid height $h$ varies from $0$ to $3.0\text{ meters}$ ($\rho_{process} = 950\text{ kg/m}^3$).

Calculate the net differential pressure $\Delta P = P_H - P_L$ sensed by the transmitter when the boiler liquid level is at $h = 2.0\text{ meters}$. (Assume $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Express Pressure at High Port ($P_H$):

$$P_H = P_{vapor} + (\rho_{process} \cdot g \cdot h)$$

2. Express Pressure at Low Port ($P_L$): (Reference Wet Leg constant height $H = 4.0\text{ m}$)

$$P_L = P_{vapor} + (\rho_{ref} \cdot g \cdot H)$$

3. Calculate Net Differential Pressure ($\Delta P = P_H - P_L$): Note that $P_{vapor}$ cancels out:

$$\Delta P = (\rho_{process} \cdot g \cdot h) - (\rho_{ref} \cdot g \cdot H)$$
$$\Delta P = (950 \times 9.81 \times 2.0) - (1000 \times 9.81 \times 4.0)$$
$$\Delta P = 18639\text{ Pa} - 39240\text{ Pa} = -20601\text{ Pa} = -20.601\text{ kPa}$$

(Note: Negative $\Delta P$ is standard for wet-leg installations; zero tank level corresponds to maximum negative differential pressure).

Problem 3: Industrial Filter Clogging Differential Pressure Threshold

Scenario: A hydraulic oil filtration unit uses a DP transmitter to monitor filter clogging. Clean filter baseline differential pressure is $\Delta P_{clean} = 15\text{ kPa}$ at nominal flow. Process safety protocols dictate replacing the filter cartridge when the differential pressure reaches $\Delta P_{clogged} = 85\text{ kPa}$. The DP transmitter has a calibrated span of $0$ to $100\text{ kPa}$ ($4\text{ mA} = 0\text{ kPa}$, $20\text{ mA} = 100\text{ kPa}$ linear mode).

Determine the threshold current signal $I_{alarm}$ that must trigger a PLC maintenance alarm.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Determine Linear Scaling Slope ($S$):

$$S = \frac{20\text{ mA} - 4\text{ mA}}{\Delta P_{span}} = \frac{16\text{ mA}}{100\text{ kPa}} = 0.16\text{ mA/kPa}$$

2. Calculate Current Output at Filter Clogging Point ($85\text{ kPa}$):

$$I_{alarm} = 4\text{ mA} + (S \times \Delta P_{clogged}) = 4\text{ mA} + (0.16\text{ mA/kPa} \times 85\text{ kPa})$$
$$I_{alarm} = 4.0 + 13.6 = 17.6\text{ mA}$$
