Chapter 11: Displacement – Ultrasonic Distance Sensors & LVDT

This chapter explores non-contact and inductive linear displacement measurement devices. Topics include Time-of-Flight (ToF) acoustic wave reflection in Ultrasonic Sensors, differential transformer coupling in LVDT Sensors, phase sensitivity, loading considerations, and numerical motor-interfacing problems.

1. Ultrasonic Distance Transducer (Time-of-Flight)

Hardware Diagram


Working Principle

Ultrasonic sensors measure distance without physical contact using high-frequency acoustic sound waves (typically $40\text{ kHz}$). The transmitter emits a burst of ultrasonic pulses. The sound waves travel through air, hit an obstacle, and reflect back to the receiver module.

Using the speed of sound in air ($v$), the total round-trip distance is $2 \times d$. The target distance $d$ is calculated from the measured Time-of-Flight ($\Delta t$):

$$d = \frac{v \times \Delta t}{2}$$

Where speed of sound $v$ adjusted for ambient air temperature $T$ ($^\circ\text{C}$) is:

$$v = 331.3 + (0.606 \times T) \quad [\text{m/s}]$$

2. Linear Variable Differential Transformer (LVDT)

Electrical Schematic Diagram


Working Principle

An LVDT is an electromechanical inductive sensor that produces an electrical voltage output proportional to the displacement of a movable high-permeability soft iron core. It consists of one primary coil ($P$) energized by an AC voltage ($V_{in}$) and two secondary coils ($S_1$ and $S_2$) connected in series opposition.

When the core is at the central (null) position, induced voltages $V_{S1}$ and $V_{S2}$ are equal, so output voltage $V_{out} = 0$. Moving the core increases magnetic coupling in one coil and decreases it in the other:

$$V_{out} = V_{S1} - V_{S2}$$
$$V_{out}(x) = K \times x \times \sin(\omega t + \phi)$$

Where $K$ is the sensitivity factor ($\text{mV/V/mm}$) and phase angle $\phi$ determines direction relative to null.

Comparative Analysis: Ultrasonic vs. LVDT Sensors

Attribute Ultrasonic Distance Sensor Linear Variable Differential Transformer (LVDT)
Measurement Type Non-contact acoustic wave reflection (ToF). Direct contact via movable iron core shaft.
Measurement Range Medium to long range ($2\text{ cm}$ to $10\text{ meters}$). Short to medium precise stroke ($\pm 0.5\text{ mm}$ to $\pm 500\text{ mm}$).
Resolution & Accuracy Moderate resolution ($\approx 3\text{ mm}$); affected by temperature & wind. Theoretical infinite friction-free resolution ($< 0.1\ \mu\text{m}$).
Operating Environment Fails on sound-absorbing soft materials; sensitive to foam/dust. Extremely rugged; resistant to harsh fluid, dust, radiation, and vibration.
Typical Applications Tank liquid level, AGV obstacle avoidance, vehicle parking sensors. Precision CNC tooling, structural strain monitoring, valve positioning.

Numerical Problems: Motion Control & Sensor Interfacing

Problem 1: Ultrasonic Distance Sensing on a Motorized Automated Guided Vehicle (AGV)

Scenario: An AGV powered by a DC drive motor travels toward a wall obstacle at speed $v_{AGV} = 1.2\text{ m/s}$. An ultrasonic sensor mounted on the front emits a $40\text{ kHz}$ acoustic pulse when ambient temperature is $T = 30^\circ\text{C}$. The sensor microcontroller detects the reflected echo after a round-trip delay $\Delta t = 14.5\text{ ms}$. Calculate:

  1. The speed of sound in air at $30^\circ\text{C}$.
  2. The exact distance $d$ to the wall at the instant the echo returns.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Speed of Sound ($v$):

$$v = 331.3 + (0.606 \times T) = 331.3 + (0.606 \times 30) = 331.3 + 18.18 = 349.48\text{ m/s}$$

2. Distance Traveled by Sound Wave:

$$S_{sound} = v \times \Delta t = 349.48\text{ m/s} \times 0.0145\text{ s} = 5.0675\text{ m}$$

3. Initial Target Distance ($d_{initial}$):

$$d_{initial} = \frac{S_{sound}}{2} = \frac{5.0675}{2} = 2.5337\text{ m}$$

4. Distance Moved by AGV during $\Delta t$:

$$d_{AGV} = v_{AGV} \times \Delta t = 1.2\text{ m/s} \times 0.0145\text{ s} = 0.0174\text{ m} = 17.4\text{ mm}$$

5. Actual Distance when Echo is Received ($d_{actual}$):

$$d_{actual} = d_{initial} - d_{AGV} = 2.5337 - 0.0174 = 2.5163\text{ m}$$

Problem 2: LVDT Interfacing with Stepper Motor Actuator & ADC

Scenario: An LVDT with a stroke length of $\pm 50\text{ mm}$ ($100\text{ mm}$ total range) has a rated sensitivity spec of $K_{spec} = 50\text{ mV/V/mm}$. The primary winding is excited with $V_{in} = 4.0\text{ V RMS}$ AC at $5\text{ kHz}$. Output is processed by a phase-sensitive demodulator with gain $A_v = 2.5$ and fed to a 10-bit ADC ($0\text{ to }5\text{ V}$ range).

  1. Calculate total sensor sensitivity $K_{total}$ in $\text{mV/mm}$.
  2. Determine the output voltage of the demodulator for a core displacement of $x = +12\text{ mm}$.
  3. Calculate the ADC resolution in terms of minimum detectable linear movement per LSB ($\Delta x_{min}$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Sensor Sensitivity ($K_{sensor}$):

$$K_{sensor} = K_{spec} \times V_{in} = 50\text{ mV/V/mm} \times 4.0\text{ V} = 200\text{ mV/mm} = 0.200\text{ V/mm}$$

2. Demodulated Output Voltage ($V_{out}$) for $x = +12\text{ mm}$:

$$V_{raw} = K_{sensor} \times x = 0.200\text{ V/mm} \times 12\text{ mm} = 2.40\text{ V}$$
$$V_{demod} = A_v \times V_{raw} = 2.5 \times 2.40\text{ V} = 6.0\text{ V}$$

(Note: Offset shift scaling can map $\pm 6\text{ V}$ to $0-5\text{ V}$ range for ADC).

3. Total System Voltage Sensitivity ($K_{total}$):

$$K_{total} = K_{sensor} \times A_v = 0.200 \times 2.5 = 0.500\text{ V/mm} = 500\text{ mV/mm}$$

4. ADC Resolution ($V_{LSB}$) and Minimum Displacement ($\Delta x_{min}$):

$$V_{LSB} = \frac{V_{ref}}{2^n} = \frac{5.0\text{ V}}{1024} = 0.004883\text{ V} = 4.883\text{ mV}$$
$$\Delta x_{min} = \frac{V_{LSB}}{K_{total}} = \frac{4.883\text{ mV}}{500\text{ mV/mm}} = 0.009766\text{ mm} = 9.77\ \mu\text{m}$$

Problem 3: Hydraulic Ram Closed-Loop Control with LVDT

Scenario: A linear hydraulic actuator moves a heavy press plate. An LVDT ($K = 100\text{ mV/mm}$) monitors position. The hydraulic ram extends at a constant linear speed $v = 25\text{ mm/s}$. Calculate the rate of change of the LVDT output voltage ($dV_{out}/dt$) and determine the voltage after $t = 1.2\text{ seconds}$ from null.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Rate of Change of Output Voltage ($dV_{out}/dt$):

$$\frac{dV_{out}}{dt} = K \times \left( \frac{dx}{dt} \right) = 0.100\text{ V/mm} \times 25\text{ mm/s} = 2.50\text{ V/s}$$

2. Core Displacement after $t = 1.2\text{ s}$ ($x$):

$$x = v \times t = 25\text{ mm/s} \times 1.2\text{ s} = 30\text{ mm}$$

3. Voltage Output after $1.2\text{ s}$ ($V_{out}$):

$$V_{out} = K \times x = 0.100\text{ V/mm} \times 30\text{ mm} = 3.00\text{ V}$$
