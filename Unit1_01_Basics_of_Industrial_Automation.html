UNIT – I | Basics of Industrial Automation and Control

1. Course Introduction

Industrial automation is the use of control systems, computers, sensors, communication networks and actuators to operate industrial processes with minimum continuous human intervention.

Simple idea: A human operator observes a process, makes a decision and operates an actuator. In an automated system: Sensor → Controller → Actuator → Process performs this operation continuously.

6. Step-by-Step Working of Automation

Step 1 – Measurement

The sensor measures the actual process condition.

Step 2 – Signal Transmission

The sensor signal is transmitted to the controller. The signal may be digital or analog.

Step 3 – Controller Decision

The controller executes the programmed control algorithm.

Step 4 – Output Command

The controller sends an output signal to the actuator.

Step 5 – Process Response

The actuator changes the process condition.

Step 6 – Feedback

The sensor measures the new process condition and the cycle repeats.

10. Learning Summary

  • Automation reduces continuous manual intervention.
  • Sensors provide information about the physical process.
  • Controllers process the sensor information.
  • Actuators perform the required physical action.
  • Feedback enables closed-loop control.
  • PLC is widely used for machine and sequence control.
  • HMI provides operator interaction.
  • SCADA provides supervisory monitoring and data acquisition.
  • DCS is widely used in large continuous process industries.
  • Industrial communication networks connect controllers, field devices and supervisory systems.
