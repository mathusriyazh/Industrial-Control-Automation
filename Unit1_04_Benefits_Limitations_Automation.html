UNIT – I | Chapter 4 — Benefits and Limitations of Industrial Automation

1. Why Do Industries Use Automation?

Industrial automation uses sensors, controllers, drives, actuators, machines and software to perform industrial operations with reduced continuous human intervention.

Main objective: Automation is introduced to improve productivity, quality, safety, repeatability and process control while reducing unnecessary manual intervention.
⚡

Productivity

More products can be manufactured in the same amount of time.

🎯

Quality

Automatic and repeatable operations reduce process variation.

🛡️

Safety

Humans can be removed from hazardous operations.

⏱️

Speed

Automatic machines can perform repetitive operations rapidly.

📊

Monitoring

PLC, HMI and SCADA systems provide real-time information.

🔁

Repeatability

The same programmed sequence can be executed repeatedly.

4. Benefit 2 — Reduced Cycle Time

Cycle time is the time required to complete one production cycle.

Cycle Time:

Cycle time = Total production time / Number of products

Manual System

Suppose one product requires:

60 seconds

per cycle.

Automated System

The automated machine requires:

20 seconds

per cycle.

Manual cycle time = 60 s
Automated cycle time = 20 s
The automated process takes only one-third of the manual cycle time in this example.

11. Limitations of Industrial Automation

💰

High Initial Cost

PLC systems, robots, drives, sensors, machines and installation can require significant investment.

🧑‍🔧

Skilled Personnel

Programming, commissioning and maintenance require trained personnel.

🔧

Maintenance

Sensors, actuators, controllers and communication networks must be maintained.

💻

Complexity

Large automation systems can become complex to design, program and troubleshoot.

🌐

Cybersecurity

Connected industrial systems introduce cybersecurity requirements.

⚠️

Failure Impact

A controller, network or machine failure can stop an entire production line.

12. Automation and Machine Downtime

14. Numerical Example — Productivity Improvement

A manual production line produces 120 units in 8 hours. After automation, the production becomes 360 units in 8 hours. Determine:

  1. Manual production rate
  2. Automated production rate
  3. Increase in production rate
  4. Percentage increase
Step 1 — Manual production rate

Production rate = 120 / 8

= 15 units/hour
Step 2 — Automated production rate

Production rate = 360 / 8

= 45 units/hour
Step 3 — Increase

45 − 15 = 30 units/hour
Step 4 — Percentage increase

Percentage increase

= [(45 − 15) / 15] × 100

= 200%
General formula:

Percentage increase = (New production − Old production) ÷ Old production × 100

15. Numerical Example — Machine Utilization

An automated machine is available for 10 hours per day. Due to setup, maintenance and downtime, it operates for 8 hours. Find the machine utilization.

Machine Utilization

= Operating Time / Available Time × 100

= 8 / 10 × 100

= 80%

Therefore, the machine has an operating utilization of 80%.

18. Chapter Summary

  • Automation can increase production rate.
  • Automation can reduce cycle time.
  • Automatic control improves repeatability.
  • Automatic inspection can improve product quality.
  • Automation can reduce human exposure to hazardous processes.
  • HMI and SCADA systems provide real-time monitoring.
  • Automation can reduce operating costs, but the initial investment may be high.
  • Automated systems require skilled programming and maintenance.
  • Industrial networks introduce cybersecurity requirements.
  • A good automation design balances productivity, safety, quality, cost and reliability.

⚡ Productivity + 🎯 Quality + 🛡️ Safety + ⏱️ Speed + 📊 Monitoring − 💰 Initial Cost − 🔧 Maintenance − 💻 Complexity

= Automation Engineering Decision
