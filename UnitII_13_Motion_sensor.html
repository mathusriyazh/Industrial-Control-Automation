1. Passive Infrared (PIR) Motion Transducer

Industrial Hardware Image


Working Principle

A Passive Infrared (PIR) sensor measures blackbody thermal radiation emitted by warm objects (humans, animals, vehicles) in the far-infrared spectrum (wavelengths around $8\text{ to }14\ \mu\text{m}$). According to the Stefan-Boltzmann Law, radiant power density $E$ increases with absolute temperature $T$:

$$E = \epsilon \cdot \sigma \cdot T^4$$

Where $\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8}\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}^4$ and $\epsilon$ is emissivity. The sensor contains two pyroelectric slots connected in voltage-series opposition. A segmented Fresnel Lens focuses ambient infrared radiation into multiple detection zones.

When a human walks past, they cross slot A first (causing a positive voltage spike $+V_{pyro}$) and then slot B (causing a negative voltage spike $-V_{pyro}$). Static background radiation affects both slots equally, cancelling out and preventing false triggers:

$$\Delta V_{out} = V_{slotA} - V_{slotB}$$

BISS0001 Processing Pipeline

The micro-volt differential signal generated by the pyroelectric element cannot directly trigger a digital controller or motor relay. Commercial modules (e.g., HC-SR501) process the signal through an integrated ASIC (like BISS0001):

  • Stage 1 - High-Gain Bandpass Amplifier: Amplifies small AC thermal pulses ($100\ \mu\text{V} - 1\text{ mV}$) while filtering out DC static drift and high-frequency electrical noise (typical bandwidth $0.3\text{ Hz} - 10\text{ Hz}$).
  • Stage 2 - Window Comparator: Compares amplified voltage against upper ($V_{THH}$) and lower ($V_{THL}$) threshold limits.
  • Stage 3 - Delay & Repeatable Trigger Logic: Holds output HIGH ($3.3\text{V}$) for a user-adjustable delay duration $T_{delay}$ to energize actuators or motors.

Numerical Problems: Motion Detection & Automated Actuator Drives

Problem 1: Radiant Thermal Power Detection

Scenario: A human body with skin surface area $A = 1.8\text{ m}^2$, skin temperature $T_h = 34^\circ\text{C}$ ($307.15\text{ K}$), and emissivity $\epsilon = 0.98$ walks into an air-conditioned room with background temperature $T_b = 20^\circ\text{C}$ ($293.15\text{ K}$). Calculate:

  1. The net radiant thermal power $\Delta P_{rad}$ emitted by the human body relative to the background environment.
  2. The peak emission wavelength $\lambda_{max}$ of the human body using Wien’s Displacement Law ($b = 2898\ \mu\text{m}\cdot\text{K}$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Net Radiant Thermal Power ($\Delta P_{rad}$): Applying Stefan-Boltzmann Law:

$$\Delta P_{rad} = \epsilon \cdot \sigma \cdot A \cdot \left( T_h^4 - T_b^4 \right)$$
$$T_h^4 = (307.15)^4 = 8.900 \times 10^9\text{ K}^4$$
$$T_b^4 = (293.15)^4 = 7.385 \times 10^9\text{ K}^4$$
$$\Delta P_{rad} = 0.98 \times (5.67 \times 10^{-8}) \times 1.8 \times \left( 8.900 \times 10^9 - 7.385 \times 10^9 \right)$$
$$\Delta P_{rad} = 1.0002 \times 10^{-7} \times 1.515 \times 10^9 = 151.53\text{ Watts}$$

2. Peak Emission Wavelength ($\lambda_{max}$): Applying Wien’s Displacement Law:

$$\lambda_{max} = \frac{b}{T_h} = \frac{2898\ \mu\text{m}\cdot\text{K}}{307.15\text{ K}} = 9.435\ \mu\text{m}$$

(This falls directly within the $8 - 14\ \mu\text{m}$ band pass filter window of the PIR optical lens).

Problem 2: PIR Triggered Motorized Automatic Sliding Door

Scenario: A PIR sensor mounted above an automatic store door has a conical field-of-view angle $\theta = 90^\circ$ and is mounted at height $h = 3.0\text{ meters}$. A person approaches the door at walking velocity $v = 1.4\text{ m/s}$. When the PIR detects motion, its internal timer holds output HIGH, turning on a DC motor drive that opens the door stroke ($L_{door} = 1.2\text{ meters}$) at speed $v_{door} = 0.6\text{ m/s}$. Calculate:

  1. The floor detection coverage radius $R_{coverage}$ of the PIR sensor.
  2. The time available $t_{approach}$ from the moment the person enters detection coverage until they reach the door plane.
  3. The time required by the motor drive to open the door fully ($t_{open}$), and verify if the person can walk through safely without stopping.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Floor Detection Radius ($R_{coverage}$): For cone angle $\theta = 90^\circ$, half angle $\alpha = 45^\circ$:

$$R_{coverage} = h \times \tan(\alpha) = 3.0\text{ m} \times \tan(45^\circ) = 3.0\text{ meters}$$

2. Person Approach Time ($t_{approach}$):

$$t_{approach} = \frac{R_{coverage}}{v} = \frac{3.0\text{ m}}{1.4\text{ m/s}} = 2.143\text{ seconds}$$

3. Door Opening Time ($t_{open}$):

$$t_{open} = \frac{L_{door}}{v_{door}} = \frac{1.2\text{ m}}{0.6\text{ m/s}} = 2.000\text{ seconds}$$

Safety Verdict: Since $t_{open} = 2.00\text{ s} < t_{approach} = 2.14\text{ s}$, the door opens completely before the walking person reaches the threshold.

Problem 3: BISS0001 Hardware Delay Capacitor/Resistor Timing Calculation

Scenario: The output pulse delay time $T_{delay}$ of a PIR controller IC (BISS0001) is set by external timing components $R_x$ and $C_x$ according to the design equation:

$$T_{delay} \approx 24576 \times R_x \times C_x$$

An industrial conveyor motor drive requires the PIR sensor output to remain active for exactly $t = 12\text{ seconds}$ after motion ceases. If a capacitor $C_x = 0.1\ \mu\text{F}$ is selected, calculate the required timing resistor value $R_x$ in kilohms ($\text{k}\Omega$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Rearrange Formula for $R_x$:

$$R_x = \frac{T_{delay}}{24576 \times C_x}$$

2. Calculate $R_x$:

$$R_x = \frac{12\text{ s}}{24576 \times (0.1 \times 10^{-6}\text{ F})} = \frac{12}{0.0024576} = 4882.8\ \Omega = 4.883\text{ k}\Omega$$
