Chapter 21: Sound Measurement & Acoustic Transducers – Microphones

This chapter covers industrial acoustic sensing, dynamic electromagnetic and condenser microphone transducing principles, Sound Pressure Level (SPL) decibel conversions, sensitivity rating metrics, and fundamental wave equation dynamics.

1. Dynamic Electromagnetic & Condenser Acoustic Transducers

Industrial Hardware Image


Working Principle

Microphones convert longitudinal sound wave pressure fluctuations $p(t)$ in air into corresponding electrical potential signals $v(t)$. In a dynamic electromagnetic microphone, incoming sound waves strike a flexible diaphragm connected to a light voice coil suspended within a magnetic field $B$.

The acoustic wave force displaces the diaphragm-coil assembly, cutting magnetic lines of flux and inducing an electromotive force (EMF) $E$ via Faraday's Law:

$$E = B \cdot L \cdot \frac{dx}{dt} = B \cdot L \cdot v_{\text{diaphragm}}$$

In a condenser (capacitive) microphone, the diaphragm acts as a movable plate in a variable-gap capacitor ($C = \frac{\epsilon A}{d}$). Pressure fluctuations change the plate spacing $d$, producing charge transfer variations $\Delta Q$ across a high bias voltage $V_0$:

$$\Delta v = V_0 \cdot \frac{\Delta d}{d_0}$$

2. Sound Pressure Level (SPL) & Decibel Metrics

Acoustic Intensity & Logarithmic Scaling

Human hearing spans an immense dynamic range from $20\ \mu\text{Pa}$ to over $200\text{ Pa}$. Sound Pressure Level ($\text{SPL}$) quantifies acoustic magnitude using a logarithmic decibel ($\text{dB}$) scale relative to the standard auditory threshold $p_0 = 20\ \mu\text{Pa} = 2 \times 10^{-5}\text{ Pa}$ RMS at $1\text{ kHz}$:

$$\text{SPL } (\text{dB SPL}) = 20 \cdot \log_{10}\left( \frac{p_{\text{rms}}}{p_0} \right)$$

Microphone sensitivity $S$ is expressed in millivolts per Pascal ($\text{mV/Pa}$) or decibels relative to $1\text{ V/Pa}$ ($0\text{ dBV} = 1\text{ V/Pa}$):

$$S_{\text{dBV}} = 20 \cdot \log_{10}\left( \frac{S_{\text{mV/Pa}}}{1000\text{ mV/Pa}} \right)$$

The open-circuit voltage output $V_{\text{out}}$ for a known acoustic sound pressure $p_{\text{rms}}$ is given by:

$$V_{\text{out}} = S_{\text{mV/Pa}} \cdot p_{\text{rms}}$$

Numerical Problems: SPL Calculations, Sensitivity & Voltage Output

Problem 1: Sound Pressure Level (SPL) to RMS Pressure Conversion

Scenario: An acoustic monitoring unit on a factory floor reads a noise level of $104\text{ dB SPL}$ near a heavy stamping press. Take standard auditory reference pressure $p_0 = 20\ \mu\text{Pa} = 2.0 \times 10^{-5}\text{ Pa}$.

Calculate:

  1. The effective RMS sound pressure $p_{\text{rms}}$ in Pascals ($\text{Pa}$).
  2. The acoustic sound intensity $I$ in Watts per square meter ($\text{W/m}^2$), assuming ambient air density $\rho = 1.2\text{ kg/m}^3$ and speed of sound $c = 343\text{ m/s}$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate RMS Sound Pressure ($p_{\text{rms}}$):

$$\text{SPL} = 20 \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{p_{\text{rms}}}{p_0}\right) \implies 104 = 20 \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{p_{\text{rms}}}{2.0 \times 10^{-5}}\right)$$
$$\log_{10}\left(\frac{p_{\text{rms}}}{2.0 \times 10^{-5}}\right) = \frac{104}{20} = 5.2$$
$$\frac{p_{\text{rms}}}{2.0 \times 10^{-5}} = 10^{5.2} = 158,489.3$$
$$p_{\text{rms}} = 158,489.3 \times 2.0 \times 10^{-5}\text{ Pa} = 3.170\text{ Pa}$$

2. Calculate Sound Intensity ($I$): Using characteristic acoustic impedance $Z = \rho \cdot c = 1.2 \times 343 = 411.6\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s/m}$:

$$I = \frac{p_{\text{rms}}^2}{\rho \cdot c} = \frac{(3.170)^2}{411.6} = \frac{10.0489}{411.6} = 0.0244\text{ W/m}^2 = 24.4\text{ mW/m}^2$$

Problem 2: Open-Circuit Output Voltage Calculation

Scenario: A measurement condenser microphone has a specified sensitivity rating $S = 50\text{ mV/Pa}$. It is exposed to an acoustic field measuring $94\text{ dB SPL}$.

Calculate:

  1. The RMS pressure $p_{\text{rms}}$ corresponding to $94\text{ dB SPL}$.
  2. The RMS voltage signal output $V_{\text{rms}}$ delivered by the microphone.
  3. The microphone sensitivity rating converted to $\text{dBV}$ relative to $1\text{ V/Pa}$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate Sound Pressure for $94\text{ dB SPL}$ Calibration Point:

$$\frac{p_{\text{rms}}}{2.0 \times 10^{-5}} = 10^{\frac{94}{20}} = 10^{4.7} = 50,118.72$$
$$p_{\text{rms}} = 50,118.72 \times 2.0 \times 10^{-5}\text{ Pa} = 1.002\text{ Pa} \approx 1.0\text{ Pa}$$

2. Calculate RMS Voltage Output ($V_{\text{rms}}$):

$$V_{\text{rms}} = S \cdot p_{\text{rms}} = 50\text{ mV/Pa} \times 1.0\text{ Pa} = 50\text{ mV} = 0.050\text{ V}$$

3. Calculate Sensitivity in $\text{dBV}$:

$$S_{\text{dBV}} = 20 \cdot \log_{10}\left( \frac{50\text{ mV/Pa}}{1000\text{ mV/Pa}} \right) = 20 \cdot \log_{10}(0.05) = 20 \times (-1.301) = -26.02\text{ dBV re 1 V/Pa}$$

Problem 3: Acoustic Wavelength and Diaphragm Dimensions

Scenario: An acoustic test bench analyzes an industrial machine humming at a fundamental frequency $f = 2.5\text{ kHz}$ ($2500\text{ Hz}$) in air where speed of sound $c = 340\text{ m/s}$. Determine:

  1. The acoustic wavelength $\lambda$ of the sound wave in air.
  2. The phase difference $\Delta \phi$ in degrees between two measurement points spaced $d = 6.8\text{ cm}$ ($0.068\text{ m}$) apart along the wave propagation vector.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate Acoustic Wavelength ($\lambda$):

$$\lambda = \frac{c}{f} = \frac{340\text{ m/s}}{2500\text{ Hz}} = 0.136\text{ m} = 13.6\text{ cm}$$

2. Calculate Phase Shift ($\Delta \phi$):

$$\Delta \phi = \left(\frac{d}{\lambda}\right) \times 360^\circ = \left(\frac{0.068\text{ m}}{0.136\text{ m}}\right) \times 360^\circ = 0.50 \times 360^\circ = 180^\circ$$
