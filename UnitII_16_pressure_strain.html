Chapter 16: Pressure Measurement Transducers & Strain Gauges

This chapter explores industrial fluid pressure measurement, diaphragm mechanical deflection, piezoresistive strain gauge sensors, Gauge Factors (GF), full-bridge Wheatstone signal conditioning, and temperature compensation techniques.

1. Piezoresistive Strain Gauge Pressure Transducer

Industrial Hardware Image


Working Principle

A strain gauge pressure transducer converts applied fluid pressure into a proportional electrical signal. When pressurized media contacts the elastic isolation diaphragm, the diaphragm deforms physically. Foil or semiconductor strain gauges bonded to the reverse side of the diaphragm experience fractional dimensional change ($\Delta L / L$).

The mechanical deformation alters the strain gauge resistance according to its fundamental sensitivity characteristic, known as the Gauge Factor ($GF$):

$$GF = \frac{\Delta R / R_0}{\Delta L / L} = \frac{\Delta R / R_0}{\varepsilon}$$

Where $R_0$ is the nominal unstrained resistance (typically $120\ \Omega$ or $350\ \Omega$), $\Delta R$ is the resistance change, $\varepsilon$ is mechanical strain ($\text{m/m}$ or $\mu\varepsilon$), and Poisson's ratio plus piezoresistive effects define $GF$ (typically $GF \approx 2.0$ for metallic foil and $GF \approx 100-150$ for semiconductor gauges).

2. Signal Conditioning: Wheatstone Bridge Configurations

Bridge Circuit Topologies

Because resistance changes in metallic foil strain gauges are extremely small ($\Delta R \ll 1\ \Omega$), bridge circuits are required to convert resistance variation into a measurable millivolt voltage signal.

For a **Full-Bridge** arrangement (two arms in tension $+ \varepsilon$, two arms in compression $- \varepsilon$), the output signal is maximized and naturally temperature-compensated:

$$V_{OUT} = V_S \times \left( \frac{\Delta R}{R_0} \right) = V_S \times GF \times \varepsilon$$

Where $V_S$ is the bridge excitation voltage, $GF$ is the gauge factor, and $\varepsilon$ is the strain induced by fluid pressure.

Numerical Problems: Strain Gauge & Pressure Calculations

Problem 1: Gauge Factor and Resistance Shift Calculation

Scenario: A metallic foil strain gauge with an unstrained nominal resistance $R_0 = 120.0\ \Omega$ and a Gauge Factor $GF = 2.05$ is bonded to a diaphragm. When a hydraulic fluid pressure of $10\text{ bar}$ is applied, the diaphragm undergoes a tensile axial strain of $\varepsilon = 800\ \mu\varepsilon$ ($800 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m/m}$). Calculate:

  1. The fractional resistance change $\Delta R / R_0$.
  2. The absolute resistance change $\Delta R$ in ohms ($\Omega$).
  3. The strained gauge resistance $R_{\text{strained}}$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate Fractional Resistance Change ($\Delta R / R_0$):

$$GF = \frac{\Delta R / R_0}{\varepsilon} \implies \frac{\Delta R}{R_0} = GF \times \varepsilon$$
$$\frac{\Delta R}{R_0} = 2.05 \times (800 \times 10^{-6}) = 1.64 \times 10^{-3} = 0.00164$$

2. Calculate Absolute Resistance Change ($\Delta R$):

$$\Delta R = R_0 \times 0.00164 = 120.0\ \Omega \times 0.00164 = 0.1968\ \Omega$$

3. Calculate Strained Gauge Resistance ($R_{\text{strained}}$):

$$R_{\text{strained}} = R_0 + \Delta R = 120.0\ \Omega + 0.1968\ \Omega = 120.1968\ \Omega$$

Problem 2: Full-Bridge Strain Gauge Pressure Sensor Voltage Signal

Scenario: A pressure sensor incorporates a 4-active arm full-bridge strain gauge layout bonded to a stainless steel diaphragm ($R_1 = R_3 = R_0 + \Delta R$ in tension, $R_2 = R_4 = R_0 - \Delta R$ in compression). The nominal gauge resistance is $350\ \Omega$, $GF = 2.0$, and the bridge is excited with $V_S = 10.0\text{ V DC}$. Full-scale pressure induces a strain magnitude $|\varepsilon| = 1200\ \mu\varepsilon$. Calculate:

  1. The full-scale output voltage $V_{OUT}$ of the bridge circuit.
  2. The pressure sensor sensitivity rating in $\text{mV/V}$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate Full-Bridge Output Voltage ($V_{OUT}$):

$$V_{OUT} = V_S \times GF \times \varepsilon$$
$$V_{OUT} = 10.0\text{ V} \times 2.0 \times (1200 \times 10^{-6}) = 10.0 \times 0.0024 = 0.024\text{ V} = 24.0\text{ mV}$$

2. Calculate Sensitivity Rating ($\text{mV/V}$):

$$\text{Sensitivity} = \frac{V_{OUT}}{V_S} = \frac{24.0\text{ mV}}{10.0\text{ V}} = 2.40\text{ mV/V}$$

Problem 3: Mechanical Diaphragm Stress to Strain Conversion

Scenario: A circular flat diaphragm pressure sensor element made of stainless steel ($E = 200\text{ GPa} = 200 \times 10^9\text{ N/m}^2$, Poisson's ratio $\nu = 0.3$) experiences a tangential surface stress $\sigma = 150\text{ MPa}$ ($150 \times 10^6\text{ N/m}^2$) under peak working pressure. Calculate the induced mechanical strain $\varepsilon$ and the resulting output of a semiconductor strain gauge with $GF = 110$ excited at $V_S = 5\text{ V}$ in a quarter-bridge configuration.

Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Calculate Induced Strain ($\varepsilon$): Using Hooke's Law ($\sigma = E \cdot \varepsilon$):

$$\varepsilon = \frac{\sigma}{E} = \frac{150 \times 10^6\text{ N/m}^2}{200 \times 10^9\text{ N/m}^2} = 7.5 \times 10^{-4} = 750\ \mu\varepsilon$$

2. Calculate Quarter-Bridge Output Voltage ($V_{OUT}$): For a quarter-bridge layout ($V_{OUT} \approx \frac{1}{4} V_S \cdot GF \cdot \varepsilon$):

$$V_{OUT} = \frac{1}{4} \times 5.0\text{ V} \times 110 \times (750 \times 10^{-6})$$
$$V_{OUT} = 1.25 \times 110 \times 0.00075 = 0.103125\text{ V} = 103.13\text{ mV}$$
