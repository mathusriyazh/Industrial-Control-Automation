UNIT – I | Chapter 3 — Types of Industrial Automation

1. Types of Industrial Automation

Industrial automation can be classified according to the production volume, product variety, flexibility required and method used to control the production equipment.

The three major categories are:

1. Fixed Automation
2. Programmable Automation
3. Flexible Automation
🏭

Fixed Automation

Special-purpose equipment performs a fixed sequence of operations.

High volume + Low variety
🖥️

Programmable Automation

The production sequence can be changed by modifying the control program.

Medium volume + Medium variety
🤖

Flexible Automation

The system can produce different products with little manual intervention.

Medium volume + High variety

3. Fixed Automation

Fixed automation is also called hard automation. The equipment is designed to perform a predetermined sequence of operations.

Characteristics

  • Dedicated equipment
  • Fixed sequence
  • High production rate
  • Low product variety
  • High initial investment
  • Low flexibility

Typical Applications

  • Automotive transfer lines
  • Bottling plants
  • Assembly lines
  • High-volume machining
  • Continuous production
Example: An automobile component production line may perform drilling, cutting, machining and inspection in a fixed sequence for thousands of identical components.

Limitation: If the product design changes significantly, changing a fixed automation system can be expensive and time-consuming.

4. Programmable Automation

Programmable automation uses a programmable controller or computer-based control system. The production sequence can be changed by changing the program.

Characteristics

  • Program-controlled operation
  • Suitable for batch production
  • Medium production volume
  • Higher product variety
  • Program can be modified
  • PLC/CNC commonly used

Examples

  • CNC machining
  • PLC-controlled machines
  • Batch processing
  • Industrial packaging
  • Automatic testing systems
Example: A CNC machine can manufacture one component using one program and another component by loading a different program.

5. Flexible Automation

Flexible automation is designed to manufacture different products with minimal manual intervention.

Characteristics

  • High flexibility
  • Automatic product changeover
  • Computer-based supervisory control
  • Robots may be used
  • Industrial communication
  • Suitable for high product variety

Typical Technologies

  • Industrial robots
  • PLC
  • CNC
  • Machine vision
  • Industrial Ethernet
  • SCADA/MES integration
Example: A robotic manufacturing cell can automatically change its operation according to the product identification and production program.

10. How Should an Engineer Select the Automation Type?

Determine production volume.

Engineering principle: There is no single "best" automation type. The correct choice depends on production volume, product variety, changeover requirement, investment, safety, quality and operating cost.

11. Numerical Example

Production Rate Comparison

A manually operated production line produces 120 units in 8 hours. An automated line produces 360 units in 8 hours. Determine the increase in production rate.

Manual production rate:

Production rate = 120 / 8

= 15 units/hour
Automated production rate:

Production rate = 360 / 8

= 45 units/hour
Increase in production rate:

45 − 15 = 30 units/hour

Percentage increase:

[(45 − 15) / 15] × 100

= 200%

14. Advantages and Limitations

Fixed Automation

Advantages
  • Very high production
  • Excellent repeatability
  • Low unit cost at high volume
Limitations
  • Low flexibility
  • Expensive modification

Programmable Automation

Advantages
  • Program can be changed
  • Suitable for batches
  • Good flexibility
Limitations
  • Changeover time
  • Programming required

Flexible Automation

Advantages
  • High product variety
  • Rapid changeover
  • Reduced manual intervention
Limitations
  • High initial cost
  • Complex control
  • Skilled maintenance required

15. Chapter Summary

  • Fixed automation is designed for high-volume, low-variety production.
  • Programmable automation allows the production sequence to be changed through programs.
  • Flexible automation can produce different products with minimal manual intervention.
  • PLC and CNC systems are commonly associated with programmable automation.
  • Robots, PLCs, machine vision and industrial networks are important technologies in flexible automation.
  • Production volume and product variety are important factors in selecting an automation system.
  • Higher flexibility normally requires more sophisticated control and system architecture.
One-line memory trick:

🏭 Fixed → Same product, very high volume

🖥️ Programmable → Change program for different batches

🤖 Flexible → Different products with rapid automatic changeover
